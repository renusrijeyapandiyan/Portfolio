The problem

Finding the right blood donor at the right time is often a scramble across phone calls, WhatsApp groups, and hospital notice boards. This system gives donors, patients, and hospitals a shared, structured place to coordinate — with hospitals able to verify and approve requests in the loop.

Key features

▸Three role-based dashboards: donor, patient, hospital
▸Full request lifecycle — pending → approved → fulfilled
▸Government ID verification for donor/patient identity
▸Live map of nearby donors and hospitals via Leaflet
▸Hospital-side blood stock tracking by group (A+, O-, etc.)
▸In-app feedback channel to the admin from every role

My role

Designed and built the entire system solo — the Express/Node.js API, the MongoDB schema for donors, patients, hospitals, and requests, the JWT-based auth and role separation, the Leaflet map integration for geolocation, and the Multer-based ID document upload flow.

Challenges & what I learned

Modeling a three-sided approval workflow (donor availability, patient need, hospital approval) cleanly in MongoDB took a few iterations — I ended up with a single Request document that tracks status transitions rather than separate collections per role, which simplified the dashboards considerably. Wiring up Leaflet with live geolocation also taught me a lot about handling location permissions and fallback states gracefully.

Known limitation: ID proof uploads are stored on local disk, which gets cleared on Render's free-tier redeploys. Uploaded documents may not persist between deploys on the live demo — this is documented in the repo and is on the list to move to cloud storage.
